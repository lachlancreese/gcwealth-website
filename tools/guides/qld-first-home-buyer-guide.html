Buying your first home in Queensland

1. Start with your numbers

Before you look at properties, it helps to know three things: how much you could borrow, how much you'll need upfront, and which government help you qualify for. A broker can work through all three with you and arrange pre-approval, so you can make offers with confidence.

  • Borrowing power depends on your income, living expenses, debts and the lender's own policies. Lenders test your repayments at a higher rate than you'd actually pay (see section 4).
  • Upfront money covers your deposit and the costs of buying (see section 3).
  • Government help can cut both (see section 2).

2. Government help for Queensland first home buyers

First Home Owner Grant: $30,000 for new homes

Eligible first home buyers can receive $30,000 towards buying or building a new home in Queensland valued at less than $750,000, including land. The Queensland Government has confirmed the $30,000 grant continues for eligible contracts signed from 1 July 2026. There's no grant for established homes.

Transfer (stamp) duty concessions

  • New homes and vacant land: since 1 May 2025, eligible first home buyers of a new home, or of vacant land to build their first home on, can claim a full concession that reduces transfer duty to nil, with no price cap.
  • Established homes: the first home concession removes duty on homes valued up to $700,000, then reduces in steps until it ends at $800,000.
  • Eligibility: buyers need to be Australian citizens, permanent residents or specified foreign retirees, and must move in within the required time.

Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme

The government guarantees part of your loan, so eligible first home buyers can buy with a deposit from 5% without paying lenders mortgage insurance. Since 1 October 2025 there are no income limits and no cap on places. Property price caps apply, and the Gold Coast is one of the regional centres with a higher cap. You apply through a participating lender, which a broker can arrange.

Can you combine them? Often, yes. The grant, the duty concessions and the 5% Deposit Scheme are separate programs with their own rules, so check each one before you sign. Official sources: qld.gov.au (first home owner grant), qro.qld.gov.au (transfer duty) and housingaustralia.gov.au (5% Deposit Scheme).

3. The costs to plan for

Before settlement

  • Deposit
  • Transfer duty, unless a concession covers it
  • Conveyancing or legal fees
  • Building and pest inspections
  • Lender fees, such as application or valuation fees
  • Government fees to register the transfer and mortgage

Easy to forget

  • Lenders mortgage insurance, if your deposit is under 20% and no scheme or guarantee applies
  • Building insurance, which lenders require to be in place from settlement
  • Moving costs and connecting utilities
  • Council rates and water adjustments at settlement
  • A buffer for the unexpected

4. How lenders assess you

  • The buffer: APRA requires banks to test your repayments at a rate at least 3 percentage points above the loan's actual rate.
  • Living expenses: lenders use the higher of what you tell them and their own benchmarks, so keep your spending steady in the months before you apply.
  • Credit cards and buy now pay later: lenders count your limits, not just what you owe. Reducing or closing unused limits can help.
  • Genuine savings: most lenders like to see savings built up over at least a few months.
  • Debt-to-income: since February 2026, banks can only do a limited share of lending to borrowers whose debts are six or more times their income.

5. Step by step

  1. Talk to a broker about your budget, your options and the help you qualify for.
  2. Get pre-approval. It usually lasts around 90 days, depending on the lender.
  3. Find a property and make an offer. For private sales, make the contract subject to finance and building and pest. Auctions are unconditional.
  4. Formal approval: the lender values the property and finalises your loan.
  5. Settlement: your conveyancer and lender complete the purchase, and you get the keys.

6. Your document checklist

  • Photo ID (driver's licence or passport)
  • Two recent payslips
  • Tax returns and notices of assessment, if self-employed
  • Three months of bank statements, including savings
  • Statements for any loans, credit cards or buy now pay later
  • HECS-HELP balance, if you have one
  • Details of any gift towards your deposit
  • The contract of sale, once you've found a property
