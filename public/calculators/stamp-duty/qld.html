QLD transfer duty rates for 2026-27

Duty is charged on the dutiable value of the property: usually the higher of the price and its market value. Each band's rate applies only to the part of the value inside that band.

First home buyers in Queensland

First home buyers of an established home can claim a concession that removes duty on homes valued up to $700,000, reducing in steps until it ends at $800,000. Since 1 May 2025, first home buyers of a new home, or vacant land to build one, can claim a full concession with no price cap. From 1 August 2026, home concessions are generally only available to Australian citizens, permanent residents and specified foreign retirees. The $30,000 First Home Owner Grant continues for eligible new homes valued under $750,000.

What isn't included

  • Additional foreign acquirer duty (an extra 8% of the value).
  • Government fees to register the transfer and your mortgage. Add them under "Registration and other".
  • Lenders mortgage insurance, if you're borrowing more than about 80% of the value.

Rates and concessions from Queensland Revenue Office, checked 17 November 2026. Your conveyancer or solicitor confirms the duty before settlement.

Stamp duty in other states and territories

  • New South WalesFirst home buyers: No duty up to $800,000; reduced up to $1 million
  • VictoriaFirst home buyers: No duty up to $600,000; reduced up to $750,000
  • Western AustraliaFirst home buyers: No duty up to $600,000; reduced up to $800,000
  • South AustraliaFirst home buyers: No duty on a new home; none on established homes
  • TasmaniaFirst home buyers: No concession (the established-home exemption ended 30 June 2026)
  • Australian Capital TerritoryFirst home buyers: No duty if you haven't owned property in five years
  • Northern TerritoryFirst home buyers: No duty concession; grants and a house and land exemption
