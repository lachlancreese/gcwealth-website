SMSF property loans

A self-managed super fund can borrow to buy residential or commercial property under strict rules. We arrange the finance, and work alongside your accountant and financial adviser.

Get advice first. We arrange SMSF finance; we don't advise whether your fund should buy property. That's a decision for you with a licensed financial adviser and your SMSF accountant. Be wary of anyone who suggests setting up an SMSF just to buy property.

How SMSF borrowing works

SMSFs borrow through a limited recourse borrowing arrangement (LRBA). In short:

  • The property is held in a separate holding trust (often called a bare trust) until the loan is repaid, then transfers to the fund.
  • If the loan isn't repaid, the lender's claim is limited to that property, not the fund's other assets.
  • The loan must be for a single asset. Borrowed money can be used for repairs and maintenance, but not for improvements that change the property's character.
  • Rent and your fund's contributions are used to make the repayments.

Residential or commercial property

Residential: the property can't be lived in or rented by fund members or their relatives. It must be a genuine investment for the fund.

Commercial: business premises can be leased to a related business, such as your own, at market rent and on commercial terms. This is a common reason business owners consider SMSF borrowing. The rules are detailed, so get advice before going ahead.

What lenders look for

  • A trust deed that allows the fund to borrow
  • The trustee structure, with many lenders preferring a company as trustee
  • Enough money left in the fund after the purchase
  • An investment strategy that supports the purchase
  • Rental income and contributions that cover the repayments

Refinancing an SMSF loan

If your fund already has a property loan, it may be worth comparing it with what's available now. Rates and features for SMSF loans vary widely between lenders. We count switching costs and any changes to the holding trust before recommending a move.

Costs to expect

Setting up the holding trust and the legal documents adds cost, and SMSF loans often have higher rates and fees than standard home loans. Some SMSF applications involve a broker fee, and we'll always tell you about it upfront.

Questions about SMSF loans

Can I live in a property my SMSF owns?

No. A residential property owned by your SMSF can't be lived in or rented by you, other fund members or related parties.

Can my SMSF buy my business premises?

Often it can, if the property qualifies as business real property and is leased to your business at market rent on commercial terms. Your adviser and accountant should confirm the details for your fund.

Can I renovate an SMSF property using the loan?

Borrowed money can pay for repairs and maintenance, but not improvements. Improvements can sometimes be paid from the fund's own cash, within the rules. Check with your adviser first.
