Construction loans

Building is financed differently from buying. The lender releases money in stages as your build progresses, so the loan needs to be set up around your contract and your builder.

Get started Call 0422 740 697

At a glance

  • Funds released in stages as the build progresses
  • Interest generally charged only on what's been drawn
  • House and land packages, knock-down rebuilds, or your own block
  • First home buyers may qualify for the $30,000 grant

Last updated 17 November 2026

How progress payments work

Your builder invoices at set stages. The lender checks progress, sometimes with a valuer's inspection, then pays the builder. Typical stages are:

  1. Deposit
  2. Base or slab
  3. Frame
  4. Enclosed (lock-up)
  5. Fixing
  6. Practical completion

During the build you usually pay interest only on the amount drawn so far. Once the home is finished, the loan converts to normal repayments.

What lenders need

  • A fixed-price building contract with a licensed builder
  • Council-approved plans and specifications
  • Builder's insurance, including QBCC home warranty insurance where it applies in Queensland
  • The land contract or title
  • Quotes for any work outside the building contract, such as landscaping or pools

Things to plan for

  • Variations and extras. Changes during the build often have to be paid from your own funds before the lender releases later payments.
  • Holding costs. You may be paying rent and loan interest at the same time while you build.
  • The valuation. The lender values the home "as if complete". If it comes in lower than expected, you may need a bigger contribution.
  • Deadlines. Lenders, grants and concessions can all set timeframes for starting and finishing the build.

House and land packages

For house and land, there are usually two contracts: one for the land and one for the build. The land may settle first and the construction loan follows. We work with you and your builder so the timing of both lines up.

Questions about construction loans

Do I pay interest on the full loan while I build?

Generally no. You pay interest on the amount the lender has paid out so far, which grows as each stage is released.

What if the build costs more than the contract?

Most lenders want you to pay for extra costs yourself, or to apply to increase the loan before the next payment. A buffer in your budget for variations is wise.

Can I get a construction loan as an owner-builder?

Some lenders consider owner-builder finance, but options are limited and the criteria are stricter. Talk to us early if this is your plan.
