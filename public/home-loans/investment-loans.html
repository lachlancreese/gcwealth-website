Investment property loans

The structure matters as much as the rate. We set up investment loans with your next purchase, your cash flow and your tax position in mind, and work alongside your accountant.

  • Lenders compared on how they treat rental income

Structure first

How an investment loan is set up affects your cash flow, your records at tax time, and how easily you can buy or sell later. We usually look at:

  • Keeping investment and home debt separate, so it's clear what each dollar was borrowed for.
  • Separate loan splits for the deposit and the purchase, where that helps.
  • Offset or redraw. They look similar but can be treated differently for tax. Check with your accountant before choosing.
  • Avoiding properties being tied together as security for each other where possible, so selling one doesn't mean reworking every loan.

Using the equity in your home

Equity is the difference between your property's value and what you owe on it. Lenders will usually let you borrow against part of it, often up to 80% of the value without lenders mortgage insurance. That borrowed equity can cover the deposit and costs on an investment property, so you may not need cash savings to buy.

We can start with a current valuation estimate, so you know how much usable equity you have before you look.

How lenders assess investors

  • Lenders usually count only part of the expected rent, to allow for vacancies and costs.
  • Your existing debts are assessed at a higher rate than you actually pay, as a buffer.
  • Interest-only periods are limited, and the rate is often higher than principal and interest.
  • Each lender's policy differs, which is where comparing them makes the biggest difference.

Working with your other advisers

We regularly work alongside accountants, buyer's agents and financial planners. With your permission, we'll talk to yours so your finance, ownership structure and tax position line up.

Questions about investment loans

Should I choose interest-only or principal and interest?

Interest-only keeps repayments lower for a set period, which can help cash flow, but you don't pay down the loan and the rate is often higher. Principal and interest builds equity faster. The right choice depends on your plans and other debts, and it's worth discussing with your accountant.

Can I use the equity in my home as a deposit?

Usually, yes. The lender values your home and lets you borrow against part of the equity. That money can fund the deposit and purchase costs for the investment.

Is the interest on an investment loan tax-deductible?

Generally, interest on money borrowed to buy an income-producing property can be claimed, but it depends on how the loan is used and set up. Your accountant or the ATO can confirm what applies to you.
