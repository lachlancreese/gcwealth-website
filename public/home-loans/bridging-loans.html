Bridging loans

A bridging loan lets you buy your next home before you've sold your current one, so you're not forced into a rushed sale or a rental in between.

At a glance

  • Buy first, sell within a set period
  • Interest on the bridge is often added to the loan
  • Assessed on the debt left after you sell
  • Plan around a conservative sale price

Last updated 17 November 2026

How bridging works

For a while you'll own two properties. The lender looks at two numbers:

  • Peak debt: your current loan, plus the new purchase and its costs, while you own both.
  • End debt: what's left once your current home sells and the proceeds pay down the loan.

The lender needs to be satisfied you can afford the end debt. You'll have a set period to sell, usually between six and twelve months depending on the lender and whether you're buying or building. Interest on the bridging portion is often added to the loan rather than paid monthly, though this varies by lender.

Is bridging right for you?

Bridging can take the pressure off buying and selling at the same time. The risk is that your home sells for less, or takes longer, than you expected, and interest keeps building in the meantime. We help you plan around a conservative sale price and timeframe, so there's room if the market moves.

Depending on your situation, there may be simpler options: selling first and renting, negotiating a longer settlement, or using equity in your current home towards the deposit. We'll compare them with you.

What you'll need

  • Details of your current loan and an estimate of your home's value
  • The contract for the new property, or your pre-approved budget
  • Payslips or tax returns, and statements for other debts
  • Your plan for selling: timing, agent and expected price

Questions about bridging loans

What if my home doesn't sell within the bridging period?

Lenders may extend the period, ask you to adjust your sale strategy, or apply higher costs. That's why it pays to plan around a realistic price and timeframe from the start.

Do I make repayments during the bridging period?

It depends on the lender. Often you keep paying your existing loan while interest on the bridging portion is added to the balance, then everything is reset once your home sells.
