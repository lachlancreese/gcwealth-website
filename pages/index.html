Common questions

Something else on your mind? Ask us directly.

Why use a mortgage broker instead of going straight to my bank?

A bank can only offer its own loans. We compare options from {{lenders}} lenders on our panel, and we're required by law to act in your best interests when we recommend a loan. We also handle the paperwork and the back-and-forth with the lender for you.

How much can I borrow?

It depends on your income, living expenses, existing debts and deposit, and on each lender's own rules. Lenders also test whether you could still afford repayments if rates rose, so the amount they'll lend is usually less than a simple repayment calculation suggests. After a first conversation we can give you a realistic range.

Do I need pre-approval before I start looking?

It isn't required, but it's a good idea. Pre-approval gives you a clear budget and lets you make offers with more confidence. It usually lasts around 90 days, depending on the lender, and can often be renewed. How pre-approval works.

What should I have ready for our first conversation?

Rough figures are enough to start: your income, regular expenses, any debts such as car loans or credit cards, your savings, and what you're hoping to buy or do. When it's time to apply we'll send you a checklist of documents, usually ID, payslips or tax returns, and bank statements.

How are mortgage brokers paid?

Usually by the lender you choose, through commission. We'll tell you how we're paid before you apply. Some complex applications, such as certain SMSF or commercial loans, may involve a fee, and we'll always tell you about any fee upfront.

Who sets interest rates?

Each lender sets its own rates. The Reserve Bank's cash rate influences them, but lenders also price for their own costs, competition and the risk of each loan, which is why rates and offers vary so much between lenders.
