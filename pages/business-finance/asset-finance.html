Your options

  • Chattel mortgage. You own the asset from the start and the lender holds it as security. The most common choice for business vehicles.
  • Finance lease. The lender owns the asset and you lease it for a set term, often with a residual value at the end.
  • Commercial hire purchase. The lender owns the asset until the final payment, then ownership passes to you.

Each option is treated differently for tax and GST. Your accountant can tell you which suits your business.

Balloon payments

A balloon (or residual) is a lump sum due at the end of the term. It lowers your regular repayments, but you'll need to pay it, refinance it or trade the asset when the term ends, and you'll pay more interest overall.

What lenders look at

  • How long your ABN and GST registration have been in place
  • The type, age and value of the asset
  • Any deposit or trade-in
  • Your credit history and, for larger amounts, financial statements

Established businesses can often finance assets with minimal paperwork. Newer businesses may need more documents or a deposit.

Questions about asset finance

Can I finance a used asset or a private sale?

Often, yes. Lenders set limits on the asset's age and may need an inspection or valuation for private sales.

Should I include a balloon payment?

It depends on your cash flow and plans for the asset. A balloon lowers repayments now but leaves a lump sum at the end. We'll show you repayments with and without one.
