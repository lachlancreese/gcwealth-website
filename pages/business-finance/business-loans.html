Types of business finance

  • Secured business loans. Backed by property or other assets, usually at lower rates and for larger amounts.
  • Unsecured business loans. No property security, often faster to arrange, but for smaller amounts and at a higher cost. A director's guarantee is usually required.
  • Overdrafts and lines of credit. Flexible limits for day-to-day cash flow. You pay interest on what you use.
  • Invoice finance. Borrowing against unpaid invoices, so you're not waiting on customers to pay.
  • Debt consolidation. Combining several business facilities into one. It can simplify repayments, but check the total cost over the full term.

Speed versus cost

Specialist lenders can sometimes approve and fund quickly, which helps when an opportunity won't wait. That speed usually comes at a higher cost. We'll show you the options side by side so you can decide what the timing is worth.

What lenders look at

  • How long you've been trading, and your industry
  • Financial statements, BAS and business bank statements
  • Cash flow, and how the loan will be repaid
  • Your business and personal credit history
  • Any security on offer

Questions about business loans

Do I need property to secure a business loan?

Not always. Unsecured loans and invoice finance don't need property, but they usually cost more and have lower limits. Secured loans are generally cheaper if you have security available.

What is invoice finance?

A facility that lends you a percentage of your unpaid invoices, so you have the cash now instead of waiting for customers to pay. It suits businesses that invoice other businesses on payment terms.

Will I need to give a personal guarantee?

For most small business lending, lenders ask directors to guarantee the loan personally. We'll explain what that means for you before you sign.
