How Queensland transfer duty works

Transfer duty is charged on the dutiable value of a property: the higher of the price and its market value. Each band is charged per $100, or part of $100. If you'll live in the home, the lower home concession rates apply. First home buyers can get more help:

  • Established homes: the first home concession removes duty on homes valued up to $700,000, then the concession reduces in steps until it ends at $800,000.
  • New homes and vacant land to build a first home: since 1 May 2025, eligible first home buyers can claim a full concession that reduces duty to nil, with no price cap.
  • Eligibility: home concessions require buyers to be Australian citizens, permanent residents or specified foreign retirees, and you'll need to move in within a set time.

Rates and concessions from the Queensland Revenue Office, checked November 2026.

What isn't included

  • The additional foreign acquirer duty (an extra 8% for foreign buyers of residential land).
  • Duty on any part of the land that isn't residential.
  • Lenders mortgage insurance, if you're borrowing more than about 80% of the value.

Your conveyancer or solicitor will confirm the duty before settlement. You can also use the Queensland Revenue Office estimator.
