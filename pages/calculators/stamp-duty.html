How stamp duty works

Each state and territory charges duty when you buy property, on the dutiable value: usually the higher of the price and its market value. The rates rise in bands, so each rate applies only to the part of the value inside its band. Some states charge less if you'll live in the home, and most have concessions for first home buyers.

What isn't included

  • Surcharges for foreign buyers, which most states add on top of duty.
  • Government fees to register the transfer and your mortgage. Add them under "Registration and other".
  • Lenders mortgage insurance, if you're borrowing more than about 80% of the value.

Your conveyancer or solicitor confirms the duty before settlement.
