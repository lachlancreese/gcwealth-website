How lenders work out your borrowing power

Lenders look at what's left from your income after tax once your living expenses and existing commitments are paid, then work out the loan that leftover amount could repay. Two rules from APRA, the banking regulator, shape the answer:

  • The serviceability buffer. Banks must test whether you could still make repayments at a rate at least {{rates.apra_buffer}} percentage points above the loan's actual rate.
  • The debt-to-income limit. Since February 2026, no more than {{rates.apra_dti_share}}% of a bank's new owner-occupier lending, and {{rates.apra_dti_share}}% of its new investor lending, can go to borrowers whose debts are {{rates.apra_dti_multiple}} or more times their income.

Non-bank lenders aren't bound by the same limits, and policies on things like overtime, bonuses, rental income and self-employed income vary a lot. That's where comparing lenders makes the biggest difference. More on the lending rules.

Assumptions in this calculator

  • Income tax uses the 2026–27 resident rates, the 2% Medicare levy and the low income tax offset.
  • 80% of rental or other income is counted, and it's taxed with the first applicant's income.
  • Credit cards are counted at 3.8% of the limit each month, a common lender assumption.
  • The new loan is principal and interest over the term you enter, tested at your rate plus {{rates.apra_buffer}} percentage points.
  • HECS-HELP repayments, dependants and lender expense benchmarks aren't modelled separately.

Questions about borrowing power

Why is the estimate lower than I expected?

Mostly because of the buffer. Lenders test your repayments at a higher rate than you'd actually pay, so the amount is lower than a simple repayment calculation suggests. Credit card limits also count against you, even if you clear them each month.

How can I increase my borrowing power?

Reducing or closing credit card limits, paying down other debts and keeping expenses steady all help. Lenders also treat some income differently, so the choice of lender matters. A broker can show you where you stand across lenders.
