Why people refinance

  • A lower rate. Lenders often offer new customers sharper rates than they give existing ones.
  • Better features. An offset account, redraw, or the ability to make extra repayments.
  • Releasing equity. To renovate, invest, or fund the next step.
  • A fixed rate ending. Your loan may roll onto a higher variable rate when the fixed period finishes.
  • Consolidating debts. Rolling car loans or credit cards into your home loan can lower repayments, but stretching short-term debt over a longer term can mean paying more interest overall. We'll show you both sides.
  • A new structure. Splitting a loan, separating investment and home debt, or removing a co-borrower.

What does it cost to switch?

Before recommending a change, we add up what it would cost:

  • A discharge fee from your current lender
  • Break costs if you're on a fixed rate, which can be significant
  • Application, valuation or settlement fees from the new lender
  • Government fees to register and discharge the mortgage
  • Lenders mortgage insurance if you'll owe more than about 80% of the property's value

If staying put, or asking your current lender for a better rate, makes more sense, we'll tell you.

How it works

  1. We review your current loan, rate, features and plans.
  2. We compare options from our panel, including your current lender.
  3. If switching is worth it, we prepare and lodge the application.
  4. At settlement, your new lender pays out the old loan. We handle the coordination.

What you'll need

  • Recent statements for your current home loan and any other debts
  • Payslips, or tax returns if you're self-employed
  • Photo ID
  • A recent council rates notice for the property

Questions about refinancing

How often should I review my home loan?

At least every year or two, and whenever a fixed rate is about to end or your circumstances change. We check in with our clients after settlement for this reason.

Can I refinance a fixed-rate loan?

Yes, but if you leave during the fixed period your lender may charge break costs. We ask your lender for a break cost estimate before you decide.

Will refinancing affect my credit score?

Each application for credit is recorded on your credit file. We aim to apply once, with the lender most likely to suit you, rather than spreading applications around.
