How lenders look at self-employed income

Most lenders ask for your last two years of personal and business tax returns and notices of assessment. They then work out your income, and some will add back items that reduce your taxable income but not your real cash flow, such as depreciation, one-off expenses, or interest on debts being refinanced.

Lenders also look at how long your ABN has been registered, whether your income is steady or growing, and whether your tax obligations are up to date. Every lender treats these differently, which is why comparing them matters more for self-employed borrowers.

When your tax returns don't tell the full story

  • One year of financials. Some lenders accept your most recent year only.
  • Alternative documentation. Specialist lenders may use your BAS, business bank statements or an accountant's letter instead of tax returns. These loans usually have higher rates or need a bigger deposit.
  • Waiting for your next return. Sometimes the better move is to wait a few months for a stronger tax return. We'll show you the trade-off.

What you'll need

  • Your ABN
  • Personal and business tax returns and notices of assessment, usually for two years
  • Business financial statements
  • Recent BAS statements
  • Business and personal bank statements
  • Details of any ATO debt and payment arrangements

Questions from self-employed borrowers

Can I get a home loan with only one year of tax returns?

Some lenders will consider it, depending on your industry, your history and the size of the loan. We'll tell you which lenders fit before you apply.

Do I need to be up to date with my tax?

Lenders want to see your lodgements are current. An ATO debt on a payment plan doesn't always rule you out, but it needs to be disclosed and explained.

What is a low-doc loan?

A loan that uses alternative evidence of income, such as BAS or bank statements, instead of full tax returns. They suit some borrowers well but usually cost more, so we compare them against a full-doc option first.
