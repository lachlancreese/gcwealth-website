Guarantor home loans

A family member can use equity in their property as extra security for your loan. It can help you buy sooner with a smaller deposit, and avoid lenders mortgage insurance.

How a family guarantee works

Your guarantor offers the equity in their property as additional security for part of your loan. You still own your home and make the repayments. Because the lender has extra security, it may lend you more of the purchase price without lenders mortgage insurance.

Most lenders limit the guarantee to a set amount rather than the whole loan, so your guarantor knows the most they could be asked to cover.

The risk for your guarantor. If you can't make your repayments and the lender can't recover the debt from you, your guarantor may have to pay the guaranteed amount, and their property could be at risk. Lenders usually require guarantors to get independent legal advice before they sign.

Who can be a guarantor?

Usually parents, and sometimes other close family members. They need to own property with enough equity, and the lender will assess them as well. Each lender has its own rules on who can act as a guarantor.

Getting the guarantee released

Once your loan is small enough compared to your property's value, usually around 80% or less through repayments and any growth in value, you can ask the lender to release the guarantee. We'll help you check when you're likely to reach that point.

Questions about guarantor loans

What happens if I can't make my repayments?

The lender will first try to recover the debt from you. If that's not possible, it can ask your guarantor to pay up to the guaranteed amount. Talk to us and your lender early if repayments become difficult.

Can my parents be guarantors if they still have a mortgage?

Often, yes, if there's enough equity in their property after their own loan. The lender will look at their situation as part of the application.

How long does the guarantee last?

Until it's released. That can happen once your loan falls to around 80% of your property's value, or when you refinance or sell.
